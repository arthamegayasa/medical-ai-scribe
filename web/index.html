A little less paperwork. A little more room for the patient.

Medical AI Scribe turns a consultation into a SOAP note draft, and shows you which part of the conversation supports each line. You review it, correct it, and decide what goes in the record.

Try the fictional example View the source

Free and open source under the MIT License. No account. Bring your own AI key, or none for the example.

Four steps, in the order you already work

Each step stays editable. Nothing moves forward until you choose to.

  1. 1

    Capture

    Record the consultation with pause and resume, upload an audio file, or paste a transcript you already have.

  2. 2

    Check the transcript

    Fix names, drugs, doses and numbers before anything is drafted. Optional Doctor and Patient labels, and a vocabulary list for terms the model tends to miss.

  3. 3

    Review the draft

    Chief complaint, Subjective, Objective, Assessment and Plan. Each statement links to its source, unsupported ones are marked, and points to confirm are listed.

  4. 4

    Export

    Copy into your record system, or save as Markdown, plain text, Word or PDF. The draft label travels with the file.

  • Regenerate one section at a time
  • Notes in English or Bahasa Indonesia
  • Session history only if you turn it on

Where your data goes

The app runs in your browser. Your key, audio, transcript and notes go from your browser straight to the provider you chose. The site that serves the app only delivers its files.

Keys stay where you put them

A key is kept for this tab only, unless you choose to remember it on this device. You can remove it at any time.

History is off until you turn it on

If you enable it, sessions are stored only on this device, can auto-delete after a period you choose, and can be erased in one step.

The provider still sees the content

Whatever you send is processed by the provider you chose. Check its data terms, and use de-identified material unless your agreements and local rules allow otherwise.

Running it for a team

Self-hosters can keep provider keys on their own server behind a workspace access token. In that mode, audio and text pass through that server on the way to the provider.

This project does not claim compliance with any healthcare privacy regulation. Whether and how you may use it with patient data depends on your provider agreements, your institution and your jurisdiction.

Questions

What does it cost?

The software is free. You pay your AI provider directly for what you use, at their prices. A local model costs nothing beyond your own hardware. The fictional example needs no key at all.

Does this website store my patients' information?

No. In the default mode the host only serves the app's files. Audio, transcripts and notes stay in your browser and go directly to your chosen provider. Session history is off by default; if you turn it on, it is kept only on your device.

Is it approved or compliant for clinical use?

It makes no such claim. It is open-source software that produces documentation drafts. Whether you can use it with real patient data depends on your provider agreements, your institution's policies and the law where you practise.

How accurate are the notes?

We publish no accuracy figures. Quality depends on the model, the audio, the transcript and the encounter. That is why every statement shows its source, unsupported statements are marked, and the note is always labelled a draft.

Does it make diagnoses?

No. The model is instructed to record only what was said, including the assessment and plan the clinician stated. It should not add its own conclusions; if it does, the statement will have no source and be marked unverified.

Can it run entirely on my own computer?

Yes, if both models are local. Run the app locally and connect Ollama or LM Studio for the note, and a local OpenAI-compatible transcription server for audio. A local note model alone does not make transcription local.

Which languages does it support?

The interface is in English and Bahasa Indonesia. Notes can be written in either, independent of the language spoken in the consultation. Transcription languages depend on the model you choose.
